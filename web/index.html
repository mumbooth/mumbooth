1 The question

Mum had a question.

Not a big one. A private one. But in Mum’s town every window has someone in it, and whatever he asked, the whole town would know it was Mum who asked.

What this means

Most AI chats tie what you type to an account, an email or a wallet. Whoever runs the service can line up every question with the person who asked it.

2 The coin

So he went to the booth.

He opened his little red wallet, dropped one coin into the slot, and a long ribbon of tickets came curling out.

What this means

Your wallet sends ETH to the treasury on Robinhood Chain. Buying coupons is a public transaction. We store the tx hash, wallet, and coupon count — the same data anyone can read on the explorer.

3 The owl

The owl never peeks.

Behind the window sat the booth-keeper, an old owl who works with his eyes shut. Thump, thump — he stamped every sealed envelope without ever seeing what was inside.

What this means

Your browser makes random coupons and seals each one before sending it. We sign coupons blind (RSA blind signatures, RFC 9474): the server signs coupons it can’t see. Your browser unseals them and checks every signature against the published key.

4 The hook

He left his wallet on the hook.

He wouldn’t need it inside. All Mum carried through the curtain was one small ticket.

What this means

After the coupons arrive, the wallet is never asked for anything again. The coupons live in your browser, and every message is paid with a coupon — not with the wallet.

5 The whisper

One ticket, one question.

Mum whispered into the brass tube, and the punch on the wall clicked his ticket. The owl knew the ticket was real. He just couldn’t tell whose it was.

What this means

One coupon pays for one fast message; a deep message takes three. The server checks a coupon is valid and unused, and cannot tell which wallet it came from — among the N coupons issued with the current key.

Spent coupons are stored as a hash and key id only — no time, no IP, no model.

6 Home

Keep mum.

Mum walked home under the moon. The town was asleep, and the owl never learned whose question it was. Behind him the booth stayed lit, curtain open. Your turn.

Afterword, for grown-ups

What the owl can and can’t see

The story, line by line, in the words we hold ourselves to.

ItemServerIn plain words
Your purchaseSeen · storedBuying coupons is a public transaction. We store the tx hash, wallet, and coupon count — the same data anyone can read on the explorer.
Which coupon is whoseCan’t tellWe sign coupons blind (RSA blind signatures, RFC 9474). The server cannot tell which wallet a coupon came from — among the N coupons issued with the current key.
Your messagesPassed through · not storedWe forward your message through OpenRouter to Anthropic’s Claude models and stream the answer back. Every request is restricted to zero-data-retention endpoints (OpenRouter zdr: true, docs: https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/zdr). We do not store messages, answers, or conversation history.
Your IP addressVisible · not loggedAccess logging is off for all /api routes and the app never writes IPs. Your IP is still visible to the server at the network level — use a VPN or Tor if that matters to you.

TimingNot preventedBuying and then chatting right away can be correlated by timing. Coupons are valid until the key’s end date; use them later if you want distance.

Where tickets come from

Three ways to fill the booth

One coupon pays for one fast message; a deep message takes three.

i Live now

Buy with ETH

Send ETH on Robinhood Chain, get a pack of blind coupons. One coupon pays for one fast message; a deep message takes three.

ii Once the token exists

Burn for packs

Send tokens to the dead address. The server reads the burn on chain and signs a pack of coupons the same blind way. The rate is set when the token goes live.

iii Once the token exists

Hold for daily coupons

Wallets holding the minimum balance can claim a few coupons each UTC day. Still blind-signed: the claim is public, the chats are not tied to it.
